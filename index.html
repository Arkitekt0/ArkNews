How La Junta’s Historical Sites Support the Community

By Ronald Hernandez Jr. | August 16, 2026 | Local News

Economic Impact on La Junta

La Junta’s historic sites generate documented economic and cultural benefits for southeastern Colorado, supported by local data from the National Park Service. The agency reports that visitors to Bent’s Old Fort National Historic Site spent 1.3 million dollars in nearby communities in 2022, supported 17 local jobs, and contributed 1.7 million dollars in total economic output, as detailed in the National Park Service Economic Benefit Report.

Bent's Old Fort: A National Landmark Driving Tourism

Bent’s Old Fort also serves as a major historic landmark along the Santa Fe Trail. History Colorado documents that the site functioned as a central trading location between 1821 and 1880, connecting travelers, Indigenous nations, military expeditions, and commercial networks across the plains. The organization notes that the fort continues to attract visitors seeking information about early commerce and frontier life.

FOX Theater: A Century-old Beacon of La Junta

The FOX Theater contributes additional cultural value to La Junta’s historic district. Cinema Treasures records the building opening in 1912 as the Rourke Opera House and later operating under FOX Intermountain Theaters beginning in 1939. The venue was renovated into two screening rooms with 159 and 96 seats and reopened in 2001. Colorado Office of Economic Development and International Trade reports that the theater contributes to downtown revitalization through the Reimagine Destinations program. The Colorado Community Revitalization Grant program identifies historic mixed‑use cultural venues as qualifying projects for redevelopment funding.

Otero Museum's Role in Preserving Local History

The Otero Museum preserves regional records related to railroads, agriculture, military service, and early settlement. A museum guide stated, “Our warehouses are full of stuff with nowhere to put it.”

Otero Community College's Role in Regional Education

Otero Junior College opened in 1941 and expanded workforce training, academic programs, and athletics throughout the twentieth century.

Connecting History, Agriculture, and Community Identity

Visitors are encouraged to explore these historic sites and experience the cultural heritage that continues to influence La Junta.
